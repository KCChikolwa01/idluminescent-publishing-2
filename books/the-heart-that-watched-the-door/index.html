About this title

What if the thing you call being responsible is really fear?

Divine has never asked for help and never stopped watching for the moment it all falls apart. Then his marriage ends and his jobs go, and every fix he tries fades after a few good weeks. Underneath it all is a locked door, a dry basin and a lamp burning lower each year.

Across the city, Lisa waits for her own life to start. She and Divine live in the same weather, in different houses.

Then two quiet neighbors with a loaf of bread and a kettle begin to show up. They never ask for anything. Slowly, the water begins to rise.

A warm, unhurried novel about love, trust and the return of the spring. For anyone who has worked hard to be needed and never learned to rest.
